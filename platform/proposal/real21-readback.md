For every real number $p$ with $21\le p\le26$, define $N_p(v)=(|v_0|^p+|v_1|^p+|v_2|^p)^{1/p}$ for every real coordinate triple $v=(v_0,v_1,v_2)\in\mathbb R^3$, and let $C_p$ be the real supremum of the set $\left\{\dfrac{3(t^p+2)^{1/p}-3^{1/p}|2-t|}{6(t^p+2)^{1/p}-3\bigl(2|1-t|^p+2^p\bigr)^{1/p}}:\ t\in\mathbb R,\ \tfrac12\le t\le2\right\}$, with real powers and real division (division by zero, if encountered, has value zero). Then for every three real coordinate triples $x,y,z\in\mathbb R^3$, with addition performed coordinatewise, $N_p(x)+N_p(y)+N_p(z)-N_p(x+y+z)\le C_p\bigl((N_p(x)+N_p(y)-N_p(x+y))+(N_p(x)+N_p(z)-N_p(x+z))+(N_p(y)+N_p(z)-N_p(y+z))\bigr)$. There is no assumption that any of $x,y,z$ is nonzero or that their values under $N_p$ are equal: the assertion includes arbitrary unequal values, cancellation in any pair or in the total sum, and every zero-input case; if, for example, $z=0$, it states $N_p(x)+N_p(y)-N_p(x+y)\le C_p(N_p(x)+N_p(y)-N_p(x+y))$, with the analogous statements when either other input is zero, while if at least two inputs are zero both sides are zero.
